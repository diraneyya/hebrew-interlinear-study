עברית מן הערבית · العبريّة من العربيّة

Interlinear study pages for learning Hebrew from Arabic. Every word carries its pronunciation and its meaning in Arabic letters, plus its probable root, its pattern (وزن) and its morpheme breakdown. Click any word to hear it alone.

תְּפִלַּת הַדֶּרֶךְ

دُعاءُ السَّفَرِ العِبريّ — الصلاةُ كاملةً، ٦٥ كلمة

The traveller's prayer, all ten lines. Audio is synthesized word by word, so the boundaries are exact by construction. A closing section collects the forms used by contemporary settings but absent from the siddur text — a fourth verb, the feminine address, and the matriarchs.

עֲקֵדַת יִצְחָק — Genesis 22:7–10

قصّةُ الذَّبيح — بقراءةٍ يمنيّة

The Akedah, read aloud in Yemenite Hebrew — the most conservative surviving pronunciation, where ח = ح, ע = ع, צ = ص and ק is a hard g. Contains מִזְבֵּחַ = مَذْبَح and וַיַּעֲקֹד ← عَقَدَ.
Why Arabic letters? An Arabic speaker learning Hebrew should never be handed a romanization — that alphabet is nobody's home. Pronunciation is written in Arabic script, meaning is given in Arabic, and the two are styled apart because they do opposite jobs.

Roots are graded solid / probable / speculative. A word with no Arabic cousin says so, rather than being handed a translation dressed up as an etymology.
