עברית מן הערבית · العبريّة من العربيّة

Learning Hebrew from Arabic — never through a third alphabet. Two kinds of material: lessons you watch, built up piece by piece with a real pause to say each one aloud; and study pages you explore, where every word carries its pronunciation, meaning, root and pattern in Arabic letters.

الدروس · Lessons — watch and repeat

١ — حَلْقةُ الحروف

الدرس الأوّل — كيف تُقرأُ العبريّةُ بحروفٍ عربيّة

The six consonant pairs that decode Hebrew into Arabic — ש→س · צ→ص · ז→ذ · ח→ح · ע→ع · ק→ق — then the whole prayer, line by line, each one back-chained from its end with a real silence to repeat into. Comes with a printable sheet.

٢ — الآلة

الدرس الثاني — كيف تُبنى الأفعالُ والحروف

The vowel law (ā → o: سَلام → שָׁלוֹם), the seven one-letter connectors, and the prefix set א ת י נ = أ ت ي ن — the same four letters doing the same four jobs. Ends by laying دعاء السفر beside the Hebrew.

٣ — الشاهدُ الثالث

الآراميّة — كيف نعرفُ أيَّ حرفٍ عربيٍّ وراء الـ צ

A detective story in three languages. Hebrew merged ص ض ظ into one letter; Aramaic merged them a different way — so Aramaic still carries the information Hebrew threw away. Settles חֶפְצֵנוּ ← حفظ before meaning is ever consulted.

٤ — القوالب

قالبُ فاعِل — تَبني كلمةً لم يُعلِّمْكَ إيّاها أحد

One mould, built one rung at a time. You make a word in the first forty seconds, meet the dual (ـايِم = المثنّى) and the וּ you'd been singing untaught, and close by saying אֲנִי אוֹכֵל — أنا آكِل — a sentence in no prayer.

תְּפִלַּת הַדֶּרֶךְ — للغِناء معها

دُعاءُ السَّفَرِ بالعِبريّة، بترتيب اللحن وتَكراراتِه

Start here. Laid out in the exact order the melody sings it — three blocks, their repeats written out, and the wordless niggun marked so you don't lose your place. Includes a Yemenite pronunciation line beside the modern one, so you can see the ع, ح, ص and ذ that today's reading drops.

תְּפִלַּת הַדֶּרֶךְ — النصُّ الكامل

الصلاةُ كاملةً كما في السِّدور — ٦٥ كلمة

The full prayer, all ten lines — including the four the melody never sings: the blessing on the work of our hands, grace and kindness and mercy, hearing the voice of our pleading, and the closing benediction.

עֲקֵדַת יִצְחָק — Genesis 22:7–10

قصّةُ الذَّبيح — بقراءةٍ يمنيّة

The Akedah, read aloud in Yemenite Hebrew — the most conservative surviving pronunciation, where ח = ح, ע = ع, צ = ص and ק is a hard g. Contains מִזְבֵּחַ = مَذْبَح and וַיַּעֲקֹד ← عَقَدَ.
Why Arabic letters? An Arabic speaker learning Hebrew should never be handed a romanization — that alphabet is nobody's home. Pronunciation is written in Arabic script, meaning is given in Arabic, and the two are styled apart because they do opposite jobs.

Roots are graded solid / probable / speculative. A word with no Arabic cousin says so, rather than being handed a translation dressed up as an etymology.
